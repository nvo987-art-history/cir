## Overview

The Civilizational Interpretive Ratio (CIR) is a theoretical
framework developed by Nicholas Van-Orton.

It examines the structural relationship between the complexity
of autonomous systems and the human interpretive capacity
available to understand, supervise, and remain accountable
within technology-mediated environments.

The framework introduces a relational perspective on the
evolution of technological complexity and human interpretation.

Its central proposition is that technological complexity and
interpretive capacity do not necessarily evolve at the same rate.

A persistent divergence between these two dimensions may
constitute a structural state change.

## Core Concepts

The framework examines two principal dimensions:

### 1. Structural Complexity

Structural complexity includes:

- Architectural layering.
- Multiple decision dimensions.
- Emergent behavioural patterns.
- Network integration.
- Decreasing operational transparency.

### 2. Human Interpretive Capacity

Human interpretive capacity concerns the ability to:

- Construct meaningful operational mental models.
- Recognise system limitations.
- Maintain bounded predictive orientation.
- Preserve responsibility structures.
- Maintain meaningful human oversight.

## Structural Consequences

A persistent imbalance may be associated with:

- Increased delegated decision-making.
- Expansion of intermediary interpretive layers.
- Reduced direct transparency.
- Diffusion of responsibility.

These are theoretical implications rather than empirically
validated predictions.

## Analytical Scales

CIR is designed to operate across three analytical scales:

1. Individual.
2. Institutional.
3. Civilizational.

The framework is also intended to remain independent of
specific technological platforms and historical technological
paradigms.

## Research Status

Version 1 is a foundational theoretical preprint.

It does not claim:

- Empirical validation.
- A formally established mathematical equation.
- A universally applicable numerical threshold.
- Demonstrated predictive capacity.
- Peer-reviewed validation.

The framework is conceptual and theoretical.

No empirical dataset was generated or analysed in this version.
